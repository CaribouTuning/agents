# The Everlight — the arc, end to end

The spine the back half of the game is built against. Written down because
seven chunks of content have to agree with each other, and because the
answer to "would this character say that here" has to live somewhere other
than in my head.

## The thing itself

The Everlight is not a weapon, a Pokémon, or a Galactic invention. It is a
pressure under Sinnoh — something old and fundamental that has been asleep
for as long as there has been a region to sleep under. Not evil. Not good.
Load-bearing.

The three lakes are what hold it down. Verity, Valor, Acuity: three anchors,
one guardian each. The mural in Celestic says it plainly and has said it for
a thousand years — *the three are not its servants, they are its locks* —
and everybody who read it assumed it was poetry.

**Cyrus's mistake, stated once so every scene can point at it:** he read the
lakes as keys. Three keys, one door, walk through it into a better world.
They are not keys. They are weight. Taking them off does not open anything;
it lets something up.

## What already happened, and what it meant

- **Under Oreburgh.** Galactic's rehearsal. They wanted to know whether human
  machinery could touch the ancient energy at all. It could. A *fragment* of
  the Everlight came loose and took a shape — the thing the player met in the
  chamber, which had been standing with its back to that door for a very long
  time and seemed relieved. Mars, arriving four hours late: *it would never
  have come out for us.* That line is the climax's mechanism, planted early.
- **Veilstone.** Saturn says there are three of them and there is a schedule.
- **Lake Valor.** They got there first. The player arrives to a dry bowl. Not
  preventable, not reversible. This is the game teaching that consequences
  stick.
- **Celestic.** The mural. Two grey coats reading it, taking notes, a
  ninety-one-year-old woman refusing to move out of the doorway.

## Act II — the slow unsettling

The world stays a Pokémon game. People go about their lives, trainers battle,
the player catches things. Underneath, in the background of ordinary scenes:
water behaving oddly, weather nobody can account for, Pokémon near lakes that
will not settle, and once or twice a trace of pale light where there should
not be one. Nothing announces itself.

- **Pastoria / Great Marsh** is deliberately a holiday. Crasher Wake shouting,
  the marsh, a light scene for Sammy and Bandit. Then one old marsh-walker
  mentions that the lakes have gone strange before — long before anyone wore
  a grey coat. First hint that the Everlight predates Galactic entirely.
- **Canalave** is the revelation. The library holds the oldest written copy of
  the myth, and Looker finally has enough to assemble the picture: Oreburgh
  was a test, the lakes are the mechanism, Mt. Coronet is the destination.
- **Lake Verity** is quiet and personal. The guardian is not majestic; it is
  frightened. The player and Sammy both see it. That is the turn — Galactic
  are not waking the legends, they are forcing something awake *through* them.
- **Lake Acuity** is the third anchor, and they succeed. The sky changes. This
  is where the game stops being subtle.

## Sammy

Not a second protagonist trailing the player. She has been reading the same
clues and gets somewhere first: Cyrus read the mural as a schematic, and she
read it as a promise somebody made. Her argument against him is never
delivered as philosophy — it arrives as her being frightened, and staying
anyway. The shape of it: *if you take away everything that can hurt you, you
have taken away everything that could have mattered.* In her own words, at a
bad moment, badly. Bandit does the same job wordlessly.

## Lost Tower, paid off late and quietly

The grandmother who never earned a badge, travelled anyway, loved people, lost
people, and did not need to be Champion for it to have counted. Never stated
as a thesis. Her name simply turns up again where it matters, and the player
either notices or does not.

## The ascent

Mt. Coronet is the true final dungeon: Galactic machinery bolted over ancient
ruins, the ruins older and better made. They did not discover the Everlight.
They rediscovered it, badly.

At the summit Cyrus activates it and it does not obey him. The three guardians
arrive — not summoned, *responding* — and the player finally understands what
they are for. Cyrus has taken the weight off. The thing underneath starts to
come up everywhere at once.

His downfall is not the battle. It is that he was wrong about the noun.

## The crisis, and the player's actual job

Not a health bar. The lake trio are trying to hold something that needs three
anchors and has none. The player's job is to put the weight back, and the only
piece of the Everlight that ever came out willingly is the fragment from under
Oreburgh — caught or not, it answers the player, because it always would have.
Mars said so out loud in chapter one.

## The ending, in two halves

1. **The world.** Sinnoh settles. Lakes refill. Pokémon calm down. Looker
   finally has his case. Galactic scatter: some run, some surrender, most
   simply have nowhere to be. Gym leaders react in character.
2. **The two of them.** A quiet scene on an early route. No legendary, no
   villain. Sammy says something true. Bandit does something entirely normal.
   The world is still here — that is the win.

## The League comes after

Deliberately. The crisis was the world's; the League is the player's. Badge
eight, Victory Road, the Elite Four, the Champion — who knows exactly what the
player walked through to get here and says so. Then credits.

## Post-game

The Everlight was never destroyed. It went back to sleep, and it will be there
as long as Sinnoh is. It left scars: places that are not what they were, and a
few that are now worth going back to. Looker reopens the file on the members
who got away. Somebody eventually finds out what became of Cyrus. The lake
guardians can be met properly, on their terms, now that nobody is trying to
prise them loose. The creation trio belong here, in the quiet, rather than
being spent in the middle of the main story.
